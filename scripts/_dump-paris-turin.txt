Paris to Turin by Train: Take the Long Way Through Chambéry
  BOLD ["Paris to Turin by Train: Take the Long Way Through Chambéry"]
---P2 style= imgs=1
  IMG media/image1.jpg
---P3 style= imgs=0
Paris to Turin sounds like a journey between two great European cities.
---P4 style= imgs=0
But the place we’d add is the one most travellers pass straight through.
---P5 style= imgs=0
Chambéry sits at the foot of the French Alps, surrounded by the mountains of Savoie and just a few hours by train from both Paris and Turin. Once the capital of the Duchy of Savoy, its pastel-coloured old town is threaded with hidden passageways beneath a great ducal château.
---P6 style= imgs=0
Spend a night here and Paris to Turin becomes something much more interesting.
---P7 style= imgs=0
One morning you’re drinking coffee in Paris. That afternoon you’re wandering beneath the walls of the Château des Ducs de Savoie. The following day, the railway carries you through the Maurienne Valley and beneath the Alps into Italy.
---P8 style= imgs=0
Your destination is Turin — Baroque, elegant, gastronomic and still one of Italy’s most underrated cities.
---P9 style= imgs=0
And the two stops are connected by far more than a railway line.
---P10 style= imgs=0
The House of Savoy ruled first from Chambéry and later from Turin. Even the Shroud of Turin made the journey across the Alps before you.
---P11 style= imgs=0
Coming from London? You can join the same adventure by Eurostar, turning this into a completely flight-free journey from the UK too.

Why Stop in Chambéry?
  BOLD ["Why Stop in Chambéry?"]
---P35 style= imgs=0
Most international travellers don’t.
---P36 style= imgs=0
That’s part of its appeal.
---P37 style= imgs=0
Chambéry was the political centre of the House of Savoy for centuries, long before Turin became the dynasty’s capital.
---P38 style= imgs=0
The Savoys controlled territories stretching across the Alps, and their story eventually became inseparable from that of Italy itself. The dynasty would go on to provide the kings of unified Italy.
---P39 style= imgs=0
So Chambéry isn’t simply a convenient place to break the railway journey.
---P40 style= imgs=0
It provides the missing chapter between France and Turin.
---P41 style= imgs=0
The historic centre is compact enough to explore on foot, but wonderfully atmospheric: narrow lanes, pastel façades, Italianate courtyards and mysterious passageways beneath the former castle of the Dukes of Savoy.
---P42 style= imgs=0
Leave your bags and start walking.
---P43 style= imgs=0
Begin with the Elephants
  BOLD ["Begin with the Elephants"]
---P44 style= imgs=0
Chambéry’s most famous landmark is certainly one of France’s more unusual fountains.
---P45 style= imgs=0
The Fontaine des Éléphants consists of four elephant forequarters emerging from the base of a monumental column dedicated to General Benoît de Boigne.
  BOLD ["Fontaine des Éléphants"]
---P46 style= imgs=0
Born in Chambéry, de Boigne made his fortune during an extraordinary military career in India before returning home and becoming an important benefactor of the city.
---P47 style= imgs=0
Chambéry’s inhabitants have given his grand monument a rather less dignified nickname: les quatre sans culs — the four without backsides.
---P48 style= imgs=0
From here, disappear into the old town.
---P49 style= imgs=1
  IMG media/image2.jpg
---P50 style= imgs=0
Find Chambéry’s Hidden Passages
  BOLD ["Find Chambéry’s Hidden Passages"]
---P51 style= imgs=0
Don’t explore Chambéry by marching directly between monuments.
---P52 style= imgs=0
Get lost.
---P53 style= imgs=0
The medieval centre developed within a confined space and buildings were gradually squeezed together as the city grew.
---P54 style= imgs=0
The result is a network of narrow passageways known as allées, cutting beneath and between buildings to connect streets and hidden courtyards.
  BOLD ["allées"]
---P55 style= imgs=0
Some are immediately obvious.
---P56 style= imgs=0
Others look like private entrances until you realise they lead somewhere.
---P57 style= imgs=0
Duck beneath the arches. Look upwards at painted façades and wrought-iron balconies. Follow whichever lane looks interesting.
---P58 style= imgs=0
This is a city where curiosity pays off.
---P59 style= imgs=0
Eventually, make your way towards the building that explains why this relatively small Alpine city was once so important.

Château des Ducs de Savoie
  BOLD ["Château des Ducs de Savoie"]
---P62 style= imgs=0
The Château des Ducs de Savoie dominates Chambéry’s historic centre.
  BOLD ["Château des Ducs de Savoie"]
---P63 style= imgs=0
What began as a medieval fortress gradually developed into the seat of the Counts and later Dukes of Savoy.
---P64 style= imgs=0
Their territories stretched across the Alps.
---P65 style= imgs=0
That geography matters.
---P66 style= imgs=0
In the 16th century, the dynasty shifted its political centre from Chambéry to Turin.
---P67 style= imgs=0
Tomorrow’s train journey therefore follows, in a loose sense, the historic movement of Savoy power from one side of the Alps to the other.
---P68 style= imgs=0
Parts of the château are occupied by government offices today, but guided visits provide access to areas of the historic complex.
---P69 style= imgs=0
And inside is another fascinating connection with tomorrow’s destination.

Before Turin, the Shroud Was Here
  BOLD ["Before Turin, the Shroud Was Here"]
---P72 style= imgs=0
The Sainte-Chapelle of Chambéry once housed the object now known throughout the world as the Shroud of Turin.
  BOLD ["Shroud of Turin"]
---P73 style= imgs=0
The House of Savoy brought the Shroud to Chambéry during the 15th century, and it remained here for more than a century.
---P74 style= imgs=0
In 1578 it was transferred across the Alps to Turin.
---P75 style= imgs=0
Tomorrow, you’re following it.
---P76 style= imgs=0
Whatever your views about the Shroud itself, the historical connection gives the journey between these two cities an unexpected coherence.
---P77 style= imgs=0
Chambéry and Turin stop feeling like separate destinations.
---P78 style= imgs=0
They become chapters of the same story.

Where to Stay in Chambéry

Day 2: Don’t Rush to Turin
  BOLD ["Day 2: Don’t Rush to Turin"]
---P95 style= imgs=0
Don’t take the first train out.
---P96 style= imgs=0
Give Chambéry another morning.

  BOLD ["Musée des Beaux-Arts"]
---P98 style= imgs=0

---P99 style= imgs=0

---P100 style= imgs=0

---P101 style= imgs=0

Rousseau at Les Charmettes
  BOLD ["Rousseau at Les Charmettes"]

  BOLD ["Jean-Jacques Rousseau"]

Arriving in Turin
  BOLD ["Arriving in Turin"]
---P127 style= imgs=0
Turin appears gradually.
---P128 style= imgs=0
The mountains begin to recede, the landscape opens and then the city takes over.
---P129 style= imgs=0
Arrive at Torino Porta Susa and you’re in a place that many international travellers still bypass on their way to Milan, Florence or Venice.
  BOLD ["Torino Porta Susa"]

Don’t.
---P131 style= imgs=0
Turin deserves time.
---P132 style= imgs=0
It was the first capital of unified Italy, the historic home of the House of Savoy and later the powerhouse of Italy’s industrial revolution.
---P133 style= imgs=0

---P134 style= imgs=0
Turin is elegant without being showy.
---P135 style= imgs=0
Baroque palaces frame enormous squares. Kilometres of covered arcades shelter cafés, bookshops and chocolate shops. The River Po curves along the eastern side of the centre and, on clear days, the Alps form an extraordinary backdrop.
---P136 style= imgs=0

---P137 style= imgs=0

Where to Stay in Turin
  BOLD ["Where to Stay in Turin"]
---P139 style= imgs=0
For somewhere creative, sociable and completely different from a conventional city hotel, try Combo Torino @thisiscombo.
  BOLD ["Combo Torino @thisiscombo"]
---P140 style= imgs=0
Combo occupies a converted historic fire station beside Porta Palazzo, one of Turin’s most fascinating neighbourhoods and home to its huge food market.
---P141 style= imgs=0
Part hotel and hostel, part restaurant, bar and cultural space, it has a distinctly contemporary feel, with private rooms alongside shared accommodation and a programme that can include DJ sets, live music, exhibitions and other events.
---P142 style= imgs=0
It’s the sort of place where you can come back from sightseeing, order a drink and actually want to stay downstairs for the evening.
---P143 style= imgs=0
For something quieter and more grown-up without losing the independent feel, Opera35 Boutique Hotel @opera35_torino occupies a restored 19th-century neoclassical palazzo close to Piazza Vittorio Veneto.
  BOLD ["Opera35 Boutique Hotel @opera35_torino"]
---P144 style= imgs=0
Original frescoes, stucco and historic architectural details have been retained alongside contemporary interiors, with a peaceful internal courtyard at the heart of the property.
---P145 style= imgs=0
Its location is particularly good for this itinerary: the River Po, Piazza Vittorio Veneto, Piazza Castello and much of central Turin can all be reached on foot.
---P146 style= imgs=0
Two very different stays — but both feel far more connected to Turin than an anonymous international hotel.

Why Take the Long Way?
  BOLD ["Why Take the Long Way?"]
---P267 style= imgs=0
You could treat Paris and Turin as two points on a map.
---P268 style= imgs=0
But stop in Chambéry and the journey acquires a story.
---P269 style= imgs=0
You discover the city from which the Dukes of Savoy once ruled their territories.
---P270 style= imgs=0
You see the chapel where the Shroud of Turin was kept before it crossed the Alps.
---P271 style= imgs=0
You eat Savoyard food beneath the mountains.
---P272 style= imgs=0
Then you board a train through the Maurienne Valley, disappear beneath the Alps and emerge in Piedmont.
---P273 style= imgs=0
In Turin, the same Savoy dynasty reappears in palaces, squares and churches.
---P274 style= imgs=0
Even the Shroud has completed the journey before you.
---P275 style= imgs=0
France hasn’t abruptly become Italy.
---P276 style= imgs=0
You’ve watched one become the other.
---P277 style= imgs=0
And that’s exactly why we love travelling across Europe by train.
